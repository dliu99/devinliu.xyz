*Tell us what risks in AI safety you care most about.*

There are inherent challenges in the transition to a post-ASI world that we are extremely unprepared for. It’s easy to think of the utopian, post-scarcity future, but I’m concerned that despite a material wealth increase, human fulfillment in general may likely decline.

The idea of gradual disempowerment describes this trend, but its primary focus is on human control rather than the impact on the human condition. For example, we have a large number of conveniences like food delivery, short-form media, and car dependency that gratify us in the short-term but do not contribute to overall happiness in the long-term. It’s possible that rather than enjoying these conveniences in a reasonable manner, we as a society could fall into further reclusivity and unfulfillment after our obligations to learn and work disappear. 

Our obligations have implicit purposes beyond themselves. The community aspects of school and work, for example, are large drivers of overall human fulfillment. For most people in the United States especially, college is emphasized more so for its value in community and serendipity rather than the actual coursework or even the degree. When these institutional obligations go away post-ASI, what happens to these communities? What happens to the suburb, which previously funneled people to their obligations? Our entire framework for a fulfilling life needs to be rethought, and there must be people building the alternatives to fulfill this purpose.

The gradual disempowerment paper uses the word *superstimuli* to describe a future type of AI-created content (far more potent than current social media) that permanently locks humans into passive consumption. AI x-risk aside, does our content landscape today not already resemble a primitive superstimuli? Does *anyone* today truly believe they are benefitting from consuming such content?

Especially in a post-ASI world, we must build a physical world as an antidote to the digital: one that exudes beauty, serendipity, and human agency in every corner of civilization. Until we create the conditions for people to live fulfilling, earnest lives, we should be extremely worried about an AI future misaligned with true human happiness.
